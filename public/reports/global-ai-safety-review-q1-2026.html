Overview

Executive Summary

The global AI safety conversation has been dominated by a narrow set of concerns: capability overreach, misalignment in goal-directed systems, and the risks of superhuman AI. A parallel and arguably more immediately consequential safety concern has received far less attention: the systematic deployment of AI systems that are cognitively misaligned with the populations they serve.

LOG_ON AI Solution Agency's research programme directly addresses this second category of AI safety risk. Through its 5-part academic series Building AI That Thinks in African Languages, LOG_ON has produced the most precise published diagnosis of discourse architecture mismatch as a safety-relevant evaluation failure in the field.

Safety Risk Identification

The Safety Risk LOG_ON's Research Names

The risk is not dramatic. No system is pursuing misaligned goals. No agent is deceiving its operators. The risk is quieter and more pervasive: AI systems deployed for consequential use cases — medical adherence instructions, financial guidance, agricultural decisions — are being evaluated against benchmarks that cannot detect whether the explanations they produce are actually understood by the people receiving them.

Every benchmark in African NLP measures what the model produced. Not one measures whether the person reading it understood it better. The user is not in the data.
— LOG_ON Research Series, Week 3: The Benchmark Trap

The mechanism LOG_ON identifies is precise. African NLP benchmarks were constructed by translating English-origin evaluation tasks. When AI produces culturally authentic Yoruba discourse — story-first framing, proverb-closing, community-referenced validation — the benchmark registers the cultural authenticity as error. Optimization pressure then systematically moves AI systems away from the cognitive architecture that African-language users find natural and trustworthy.

This is a safety-relevant misalignment: AI systems embedded in healthcare communication are being optimized against a standard that treats cognitively appropriate outputs as errors. The downstream effect on health outcomes, financial decisions, and educational attainment is not theoretical — it is occurring at scale, right now.

Field Implications

What LOG_ON's Research Means for AI Safety

  • Every AI safety evaluation programme that operates across linguistic communities should audit whether its benchmarks were constructed from translated English. If so, the LOG_ON benchmark paradox applies directly.
  • The four new task types — proverb-completion inference, oral narrative arc completion, community validation, discourse connector identification — are directly usable by AfricaNLP and broader multilingual AI research communities.
  • The comprehension study design (Week 4) represents a template replicable for Indian, Arabic, Indigenous American, and other non-Western language communities facing the same cognitive architecture gap.
  • The community ownership question — who decides when an AI system's cultural output is wrong — must be answered before deployment, not after. LOG_ON's architecture requires community panels with genuine decision authority, not consultative presence.
The philosophical question — is it really thinking in Yoruba? — is secondary to the practical one: who owns it? Who decides when it is wrong? If the answer is the community it serves, the architecture is legitimate.
— LOG_ON Research Series, Week 5: The Architecture
