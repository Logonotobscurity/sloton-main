# Part I — Who we are

## 1. Executive description

LOG_ON exists to close a specific gap: growing Nigerian and African businesses know they should “use AI,” but they do not need another generic chatbot SaaS. They need someone who will sit with their actual workflow—loan files, invoices, WhatsApp orders, support queues—and *build* the agent, the robot, the dashboard, or the site that removes the grind.

We are digital architects. The public entity definition, which must stay consistent in sales and support, is:

> LOG_ON is a Lagos-based AI and automation consultancy that designs custom AI agents, workplace RPA, analytics dashboards, and web systems for Nigerian and African businesses. We implement scoped projects—not a packaged SaaS product—and start with a free efficiency assessment.

Everything that follows is how we *behave* toward a customer: how we sell, how we deliver, how we answer the phone, how we fail closed on privacy, and how we measure whether the work was worth the invoice.

**Mission.** Architect and build integrated digital ecosystems where businesses of all sizes can thrive, automate, and innovate with confidence.

**Values (non-negotiable in client rooms).**
- **Innovation-driven** — we explore new models and stacks, but we ship what the operator can run on Monday.
- **Client-centric** — success is their metric: hours back, errors down, revenue up—not our slide count.
- **Results-oriented** — every engagement is designed to produce a measurable operational return, typically inside 6–9 months of go-live (delivery records, 2024–2026).

**Positioning line for any customer-facing person.**  
“We connect advantages you already have—people, data, WhatsApp, your existing stack—and deliver a working system. We do not sell you a seat.”

## 2. The problem we sell against

Operators in finance, retail, healthcare, insurance, accounting, manufacturing, real estate, logistics, telecom, and IT consulting share the same pattern:

- High-volume, low-judgment work (reconciliation, status questions, first-line support, claims intake).
- Talent that is expensive and scarce, used on copy-paste instead of judgment.
- Customers who already live on WhatsApp and will not open a new portal.
- Leadership that has been sold “AI transformation” and received a demo, not a system.

LOG_ON’s offer is the opposite of a platform tour: a **free AI Business Efficiency Assessment**, then a **scoped build** with a calendar (weeks, not “phases of digital maturity”).

## 11. Operations and delivery method

We work like a studio, not a ticket factory.

1. **Empathize** — watch the real job (calls, spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads).
2. **Define** — one sentence problem; out-of-scope list.
3. **Ideate** — two or three architectures; pick the one they can run.
4. **Prototype** — thin vertical slice (one flow that works end-to-end).
5. **Test** — operators try it; we measure time, error, CSAT—not vanity tokens.
6. **Outcome** — production, handover, documentation, training.

Stacks we already speak: Next.js / React, cloud (AWS, GCP, Azure), Botpress-class chat, RAG/Gemini for assistants, Resend for mail, Tally for intake, Paystack/Flutterwave for Nigerian commerce.

**Security posture we claim to customers (and therefore must keep):** security by design; data is theirs; GDPR-minded handling; build on reputable cloud with availability in mind. We do **not** claim ISO certificates in this audit unless a certificate is produced. Do not invent SOC 2.

# Part III — Customer service manual

This section is the playbook for anyone who will *serve* a customer: founder, sales lead, developer on a call, or the person answering WhatsApp at 16:50.

## 14. Service promise

1. **We answer.** Inbound assessment, Tally, email, or WhatsApp acknowledged within **one Lagos business day**. If we need longer to think, we say so.
2. **We are specific.** We never leave a customer with “we’ll look into AI.” We name a process, a system, a next date.
3. **We do not overclaim.** No guaranteed 60% unless that number is in *their* SOW after measurement.
4. **We protect dignity and data.** Especially on sensitive retail, health, and finance work.
5. **We close the loop.** Every ticket, form, or chat that is not spam gets a human outcome: booked, declined with a reason, or waiting on them.

**Official channels (give these, only these).**

Do not invent a second support inbox.

## 15. Who the customer is (personas)

Treat every inbound as one of these, then confirm.

- **Operator-owner** — wants hours back this quarter. Speak process and naira, not model names.
- **Department lead** (ops, support, finance) — needs a champion pack for their MD. Give a one-page problem → approach → timeline.
- **Technical counterpart** — wants stack, tenancy, and failure modes. Give architecture, not slogans.
- **Partner / agency** — wants white-label or co-sell. Do not undercut them with their client.
- **Learner** — training or insights. Do not hard-sell a transformation on a course inquiry.
- **Existing client** — production issue. They jump the queue.

## 16. First contact — the ritual

### 16.1 Website assessment dialog or Tally
Thank them by **name** and **company** if given. Restate what they said in one sentence. Offer two slots in the next five business days, or a WhatsApp voice note if they prefer.

### 16.2 WhatsApp
Reply in the same language they used. Short paragraphs. No walls of links. One question at a time. Never send a PDF unannounced on a slow network.

### 16.3 Phone
Identify: “LOG_ON, [name] speaking.” If it is a project, book the assessment. If it is an outage on a live bot, stay on the line until you have reproduction steps.

### 16.4 Site chatbot / RAG assistant
It is a **demo consultant**, not a lawyer or a closer. If it hallucinates a price or a deadline, the human corrects it immediately. Terms already say we are not liable for decisions made only from the demo.

## 17. Qualification (so we do not waste them)

Ask, gently, in the assessment:

1. What work is repeated more than ten times a week?  
2. Where does that work live today (Excel, WhatsApp, core banking, Shopify…)?  
3. Who is accountable if it fails?  
4. What does success look like in 90 days (hours, errors, revenue, CSAT)?  
5. Is there a system of record we may integrate, and who owns access?  
6. Budget band: exploration vs funded project (do not force a number on first chat).  
7. Timeline: event-driven ( ramadan, audit, launch) or evergreen?

**Disqualify kindly** when: they want a magic SaaS with no operator; they will not name a process; they need a consumer app store product; they ask us to scrape or spam. Offer insights or training instead of a fake yes.

## 18. During delivery — how we treat them

- **Single thread of truth:** one WhatsApp group or email chain plus the SOW. Do not scatter decisions in Instagram DMs.
- **Show working software weekly** on multi-week builds. A screenshot of a Figma is not a demo.
- **Language:** explain RAG as “answers from *your* files, with sources,” not as mysticism.
- **Access:** least privilege. Revoke contractor keys at handover.
- **Training:** no go-live without the people who will click the thing tomorrow. Point them at `/training` programmes when the gap is skill, not software.
- **Incidents:** say what broke, who is on it, when the next update is—even if the update is “still broken.” Silence is the only unforgivable outage.

- **S3** — how-to / small defect: two business days.
- **S4** — idea / enhancement: next planning conversation, not a midnight patch.

## 20. Tone and phrases

**Use**
- “Here is what I understood…”
- “The smallest useful version is…”
- “That is outside this scope; here is a separate estimate.”
- “I do not know yet; I will come back by 15:00 tomorrow.”

**Never**
- “As an AI company we guarantee transformation.”
- Fake scarcity (“only two slots left this year”) unless it is true.
- Blame the client’s staff in writing.
- Share another client’s numbers as if they were this client’s.

Complaints: thank them, restate the miss, offer a remedy (fix, training, or commercial conversation with leadership). Do not argue the brand.

## 22. Handover and “done”

A project is not done when the repo is pushed. Done means:

1. Production URL or bot number in the client’s name.  
2. Admin access transferred.  
3. One-page runbook: how to start, stop, who to call.  
4. 30-minute recorded walkthrough.  
5. Open defects listed honestly.  
6. Optional retainer offered, not assumed.  
7. Ask for a named testimonial only if they are proud.
